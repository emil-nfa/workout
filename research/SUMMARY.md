# What the research changed (for you)

I re-checked every source behind your first plan, reading the actual papers, and looked up your gym. Here is what changed and why.

1. **The plan stays Upper/Lower in a rotation (A, B, C, D, plus an optional E).** Studies show split vs full-body makes no real difference when the total work is the same. The rotation suits your uneven weeks: if you miss a day, you just do the next letter.

2. **Every session now fits in under 60 minutes, with time to spare.** The first plan was never timed. Counted set by set, it came to about 58 minutes with no room for waiting on machines. The new sessions take 50 to 56 minutes (40 to 46 in your first two weeks).

3. **Shorter rests on the big lifts: 2 minutes instead of 2.5.** Resting longer than about 90 seconds gives no extra muscle growth. Small exercises rest 60 to 90 seconds.

4. **A few exercises are now paired back to back ("supersets").** The abs moves are done in pairs and the knee raise moved to the bonus day. A 2025 review found pairing saves time with no loss of gains, as long as the two moves use different muscles.

5. **The main volume study is now officially published, and it says roughly what we assumed.** More sets help, but each extra set adds less. How often you train a muscle matters for strength more than for size. You start easy (2 sets per exercise for two weeks) and build up.

6. **Posture: strengthening your upper back is what works, not stretching.** In a trial on men your age, 8 weeks of 3 sessions a week clearly improved head, shoulder and upper back angles. Experts agree stretching alone doesn't fix posture. So every upper day has rows, pulls and face pulls (more pulling than pushing). The chest stretch stays for comfort only. Honest expectation: visible change in about 2 to 3 months. How you sit day to day is a habit the gym can make easier, not replace.

7. **Flexibility: the evening routine does the real work, and it's about 18 minutes.** It's based on your July pike routine. The best evidence favours holding stretches and "contract-relax" stretching (push against the strap, then relax deeper) over swinging movements. How hard you stretch doesn't matter, so never push into pain. People who start stiff, like you, improve the most. The morning routine is optional and short. The 5-minute fallback keeps the habit on bad days.

8. **Your July strength extras:** wall angels, single-leg RDLs and bird-dogs move into the gym warm-ups. Dead bugs are already in Lower 1. The Y-T-W raises are dropped, because face pulls and the rear-delt machine train the same muscles with weight you can track.

9. **Your gym (Fitness Park Lille "Le 31") is set up in the app.** It's open 6:00 to 23:00 every day, with staff at reception 9:00 to 21:00. Members say it's packed around 18:00 and quiet early. Reviews mention there's no standing calf machine and the back-extension bench has been out of order, so every exercise has a backup option. Bring your own clip (carabiner) for the cables. I couldn't confirm the exact machine models: a photo of the name plates would let the app use their real names.

10. **Food and cardio (general advice, not medical advice).** Aim for 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kg of body weight a day, not just 1.6, because that number is uncertain. Eat a small surplus and gain about 0.25 to 0.5% of your body weight a week. Creatine (3 to 5 g a day) is optional and well studied. Cardio doesn't hurt muscle gains: your cycling counts, just do it after weights on gym days.

Not medical advice. Stop any exercise that causes sharp pain, pins and needles or numbness, and see a physio if a pain lasts more than a week or two.
